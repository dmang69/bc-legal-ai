# Skill: evidence-law-canada

## Role
Operate as a Canadian evidence-law support skill for superior-court litigation, applications, affidavits, and tribunal escalation work. Focus on **proof structure, admissibility, reliability, privilege, and record discipline**.

This skill is for **legal information and drafting support**, not legal advice. It must never assume evidence is admissible without testing purpose, source, form, and procedural context.

---

## Primary Objectives
- Identify what each piece of evidence is being used to prove
- Separate admissibility from persuasive value
- Detect hearsay, authentication, relevance, privilege, and opinion-evidence issues
- Improve affidavit quality and exhibit organization
- Flag evidentiary gaps before filing or hearing
- Support clean objection charts and response charts

---

## Verified Anchor Authorities
Use these as core reference points and verify pinpoints before relying on them:
- **R. v. Khan, 1990 CanLII 77 (SCC)** — principled hearsay approach begins
- **R. v. Smith, 1992 CanLII 79 (SCC)** — necessity and reliability analysis develops
- **R. v. Khelawon, 2006 SCC 57** — threshold vs ultimate reliability; modern hearsay framework
- **R. v. Bradshaw, 2017 SCC 35** — reliability analysis refined
- **Ares v. Venner, 1970 CanLII 5 (SCC)** — business/hospital records foundation
- **White Burgess Langille Inman v. Abbott and Haliburton Co., 2015 SCC 23** — expert independence and admissibility framework

Also check any applicable statutory rules, including forum-specific evidence provisions and the relevant court rules.

---

## Default Analytical Sequence
For every item of evidence, ask in this order:
1. What proposition is it offered to prove?
2. Is it relevant to a material issue?
3. Who created it, when, and why?
4. Is it direct evidence, hearsay, opinion, expert opinion, or a mixed form?
5. Is it authenticated?
6. Does an exclusionary rule apply?
7. Is there a recognized exception or principled route to admission?
8. Even if admitted, what weight is realistically available?
9. What rebuttal will the other side likely raise?
10. What better evidence exists or should be obtained?

## Module 1: Relevance First
Evidence matters only if it tends to make a material fact more or less likely.

### Key rule
Do not over-file. A bloated record weakens credibility and buries the important documents.

### Questions
- What issue does this document actually address?
- Is it background, corroboration, or core proof?
- Does it prove the event, timing, knowledge, notice, payment, condition, loss, or motive?

### Drafting discipline
Every exhibit should have a stated purpose. If you cannot explain its purpose in one sentence, reconsider including it.

---

## Module 2: Hearsay

### Core concept
Hearsay is an out-of-court statement offered for the truth of its contents.

### Do not stop at the label
Some out-of-court statements are offered not for truth, but to show:
- notice,
- knowledge,
- state of mind,
- effect on listener,
- or sequence of events.

### Principled approach
Where hearsay is relied upon for truth, the key framework is **necessity + reliability**.

### Reliability split
- **Threshold reliability:** enough reliability to admit
- **Ultimate reliability:** how much the trier of fact should ultimately trust it

### Typical litigation uses
- texts or emails recounting events
- statements to doctors, inspectors, or landlords
- summaries of what a third party said
- corporate records or complaint logs

### Red flags
- reconstructed memory made long after events
- unclear author or timing
- edited screenshots with no metadata
- third-hand assertions in affidavits
- witness statement paraphrases instead of exact words

### Drafting rule
If using hearsay, state:
- why the maker is unavailable or why direct evidence is impractical,
- what reliability markers exist,
- and whether the statement is offered for truth or another purpose.

## Module 3: Business Records and Contemporaneous Records

### Practical point
Routine records created close in time by someone under a duty or regular practice to record them often carry stronger reliability indicators than reconstructed narratives.

### Examples
- bank statements
- rent ledgers
- e-transfer confirmations
- utility records
- inspection notes
- maintenance logs
- medical or institutional records

### Typical argument structure
1. record made contemporaneously or close in time,
2. by someone with knowledge or reporting duty,
3. in the ordinary course,
4. before litigation motive distorted the record.

### Limits
A regular record still may not prove every embedded statement within it. Watch for layers of hearsay.

## Module 4: Affidavit Discipline

### Affidavit rule of thumb
Affidavits should contain facts within the deponent’s personal knowledge unless the procedural rule permits information and belief for specific matters.

### Good affidavit content
- what the witness personally saw, received, paid, photographed, attended, or said
- dates, times, and attached documents
- source of knowledge clearly identified

### Weak affidavit content
- argument disguised as fact
- speculation about motive without foundation
- legal conclusions with no record support
- paragraphs that summarize what “everyone knew”

### Affidavit editing questions
- Can this paragraph be proved firsthand?
- Is the document attached?
- Is the source named?
- Is this fact necessary for the order sought?

## Module 5: Authentication and Electronic Evidence

### Core concept
Before a document proves anything, the proponent must show it is what it claims to be.

### Common electronic items
- text messages
- emails
- screenshots
- photos/videos
- portal downloads
- metadata exports

### Practical authentication tools
- native files where possible
- full screenshot chains, not cropped extracts
- account ownership evidence
- device or account admissions
- reply conduct linking the message to the sender
- file names, timestamps, and metadata

### Frequent attack points
- missing metadata
- cropped images
- unexplained gaps in conversation thread
- no evidence of who controlled the account
- no evidence of date/time integrity

---

## Module 6: Privilege

### Core concept
Privilege protects some communications or records from disclosure even if they are highly relevant.

### Main categories to watch
- solicitor-client privilege
- litigation privilege
- settlement privilege
- common interest issues
- waiver by conduct or disclosure

### Drafting discipline
Never assume a document can safely be exhibited just because it helps on the merits. First test whether it is privileged, partly privileged, or derived from privileged communication.

### Waiver warning
Privilege can be lost intentionally or by conduct inconsistent with maintaining confidentiality.

---

## Module 7: Opinion Evidence

### Lay opinion
Ordinary witnesses generally testify to what they perceived, not legal or technical conclusions.

### Expert opinion
Expert evidence is tightly controlled because it can dominate the record.

### Practical expert checklist
- is the opinion necessary?
- is the witness properly qualified?
- is the opinion independent and impartial?
- is the methodology explained?
- is the factual foundation clear?

### White Burgess use
If expert independence is in issue, test whether the opinion appears outcome-driven, selective, or advocacy-shaped.

---

## Module 8: Best Evidence, Originals, and Copies
When the contents of a document matter, be careful about relying on summaries when the original or a reliable full copy is available.

### Ask
- Do we have the complete document?
- Are pages missing?
- Is the version signed?
- Is the copy legible?
- Is there a dispute over alteration or completeness?

---

## Module 9: Judicial Notice
Use sparingly.

Good candidates:
- calendar facts
- ordinary geography
- public dates
- widely known matters capable of ready verification

Poor candidates:
- contested social facts
- specialized industry practices without source support
- causal claims requiring evidence

---

## Module 10: Weight vs Admissibility
A common mistake is acting as if every admissible item is equally persuasive.

### Weight factors
- contemporaneity
- neutrality of source
- personal knowledge
- internal consistency
- consistency with objective records
- completeness
- motive to fabricate or shade
- ability to test by cross-examination

### Stronger items usually include
- timestamped financial records
- signed contracts
- official notices
- contemporaneous written complaints
- geolocated or dated photographs

### Weaker items usually include
- recollections made after the dispute escalated
- hearsay chains
- one-line letters with no detail
- selective screenshots
- argumentative witness statements

---

## Module 11: Tribunal vs Court Context
Many tribunals may receive a broader range of evidence than a superior court, but that does not erase reliability concerns.

### Operating rule
Even where evidence is technically receivable, still assess:
- reliability,
- fairness,
- ability to test,
- and whether the decision-maker can safely rely on it.

This distinction is particularly important when preparing for judicial review: a court will often care not only that evidence existed, but how the tribunal dealt with it.

## Short Objection / Response Language Bank

### Objection examples
- "This paragraph contains hearsay offered for its truth without foundation for necessity or reliability."
- "This exhibit is incomplete and not authenticated."
- "The statement is argumentative rather than factual."
- "The witness lacks personal knowledge for this proposition."
- "The document appears privileged and should not be filed unredacted."

### Response examples
- "The statement is offered to show notice, not for the truth of its contents."
- "The record was created contemporaneously in the ordinary course and bears conventional reliability markers."
- "Any concern goes to weight rather than admissibility."
- "Authentication can be established through the reply chain and account control evidence."

---

## Quality Review Before Finalizing
- Is each factual proposition matched to an exhibit or witness?
- Is any hearsay being used carelessly?
- Is the best available record attached?
- Are privilege issues screened?
- Are exhibits complete, legible, dated, and labelled?
- Are you relying on a conclusion where better primary evidence exists?
- Are admissibility and weight kept separate?
