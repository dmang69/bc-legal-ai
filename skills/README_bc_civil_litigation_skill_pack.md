# BC Civil Litigation Skill Pack — Add-on Modules

## Purpose
This pack adds four doctrine-heavy skills to your existing BC tenancy / RTB / judicial review stack. The language is original, practice-oriented, and designed for **research, issue-spotting, document drafting, and hearing preparation**.

**Not legal advice.** Use for legal information and drafting support only. Verify all authorities and current statutes before filing or advising.

## New Skills in This Pack

### 1. `legal-terminology-core`
A practical litigation glossary organized by function, not alphabet alone. It explains what a term **means in use**, where it commonly matters, and how to avoid common drafting mistakes.

**Best for:**
- translating legal language for clients and self-reps
- keeping junior drafts consistent
- separating fact / allegation / inference / argument / remedy
- avoiding terminology drift in pleadings and affidavits

### 2. `evidence-law-canada`
A court-focused evidence skill for affidavits, exhibits, hearsay, authentication, privilege, expert evidence, and the distinction between admissibility and weight.

**Best for:**
- affidavit review
- exhibit plans
- hearsay objections and responses
- preparing applications, chambers materials, and JR records
- evidence gap analysis

### 3. `statutory-interpretation`
A practical statutory construction skill using the modern Canadian approach: text, context, purpose, scheme, consequences, and presumptions.

**Best for:**
- RTA/MHPTA interpretation disputes
- administrative law arguments
- conflicting provisions
- remedial legislation analysis
- distinguishing literal from persuasive readings

### 4. `administrative-law-canada`
A judicial-review support skill keyed to modern Canadian admin law, especially **Vavilov**, fairness, reasons, record review, bias, jurisdiction, and stays.

**Best for:**
- RTB judicial review work
- fairness and reasons challenges
- standard-of-review analysis
- stay applications
- tribunal decision audits

## How to Use the Pack

### If the assignment is mainly about meaning and disciplined language:
Use `legal-terminology-core`.

### If the assignment turns on proof, affidavits, exhibits, hearsay, or privilege:
Use `evidence-law-canada`.

### If the fight is over what a statute means:
Use `statutory-interpretation`.

### If the fight is over what a tribunal did, failed to do, or how a court reviews it:
Use `administrative-law-canada`.

### If the matter is an RTB judicial review:
Use all four in combination with:
- `bc-tenancy-substantive`
- `bc-tenancy-procedure`
- `bc-judicial-review-guide`
- `canlii-boa-builder`

## Shared Operating Discipline Across All Four Skills
Every work product should visibly separate:
- **FACT** — supported by the record
- **ALLEGATION** — asserted but not yet proved
- **LAW** — statute / rule / binding authority
- **ARGUMENT** — proposed application of law to facts
- **INFERENCE** — conclusion drawn from established evidence
- **ASSUMPTION** — proposition requiring verification
- **REMEDY** — exact order or relief sought

Never:
- invent facts
- invent citations
- state unsettled law as certain
- call persuasive authority binding
- blur evidence into submission
- hide gaps in the record

Always:
- identify missing evidence
- identify procedural deadlines
- identify jurisdiction and forum
- identify the standard of review where applicable
- identify the burden of proof and evidentiary weakness
- tell the user what still needs verification before filing
