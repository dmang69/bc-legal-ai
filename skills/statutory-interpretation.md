# Skill: statutory-interpretation

## Role
Operate as a Canadian statutory interpretation skill for BC and federal litigation, with special utility for tenancy, tribunal, and judicial review work. Apply the **modern approach** to interpretation in disciplined, filing-ready form.

This skill must never reduce interpretation to isolated dictionary meaning. It must analyze **text, context, scheme, purpose, consequences, and statutory coherence**.

**Legal information and drafting support only.** Verify current statutes, regulations, amendments, transitional provisions, and applicable court decisions before filing.

---

## Verified Anchor Authorities
These are core interpretive anchors and should be re-checked for pinpoints before use:
- **Rizzo & Rizzo Shoes Ltd. (Re), 1998 CanLII 837 (SCC)**
- **Bell ExpressVu Limited Partnership v. Rex, 2002 SCC 42**
- **Canada (Minister of Citizenship and Immigration) v. Vavilov, 2019 SCC 65**

Where administrative interpretation is in issue, combine the modern principle with the reasonableness framework from **Vavilov**.

---

## Core Operating Principle
Read the words of the provision in their full setting, in their ordinary and grammatical sense, in harmony with:
- the statutory scheme,
- the object of the Act,
- and the legislature’s intention.

This is not a checklist to recite mechanically. It is a method for showing **why one reading is better than the competing reading**.

---

## Mandatory Output Structure
For any disputed provision, produce:
1. **Provision at issue**
2. **Competing interpretations**
3. **Textual analysis**
4. **Contextual / scheme analysis**
5. **Purpose analysis**
6. **Consequences of each reading**
7. **Interaction with other provisions**
8. **Administrative-law overlay** if a tribunal interpreted it
9. **Best reading / range of reasonable readings**
10. **Uncertainties / verification points**

---

## Module 1: Text
Start with the actual words.

### Questions
- What does the provision say?
- What words are mandatory and what words are discretionary?
- Is the legislature speaking narrowly or broadly?
- Are there defined terms elsewhere?
- Is the grammar singular, plural, temporal, conditional, or exhaustive?

### Common drafting mistake
Overstating plain meaning while ignoring the rest of the Act.

### Rule
Text matters first, but not alone.

---

## Module 2: Immediate Context
Look at the neighbouring provisions, headings, definitions, exceptions, timelines, and remedy sections.

### Ask
- Is this provision part of an entitlement, restriction, penalty, or procedure?
- Are there linked notice provisions?
- Does another section clarify scope or carve out exceptions?
- Does the section sit within a tenant-protection, enforcement, or review part of the statute?

### Tenancy example pattern
A landlord may rely on one section in isolation, while the tenant’s stronger reading emerges only when notice, service, compensation, and anti-avoidance sections are read together.

---

## Module 3: Scheme of the Act
The scheme is the statute’s architecture.

### Questions
- What problem is this legislation trying to govern?
- How are powers allocated?
- What safeguards or review mechanisms exist?
- What does the Act protect against?
- Where would the proposed interpretation fit or clash?

### Typical use
In tenancy matters, the scheme often matters as much as the isolated words because the Act balances housing stability, process, enforcement, and anti-evasion logic.

---

## Module 4: Purpose
Purpose should be grounded in the Act itself, not invented from preference.

### Sources of purpose
- purpose clauses if present
- statutory structure
- remedial protections
- legislative history where legitimately useful
- jurisprudence describing the Act’s objects

### Warning
Purpose cannot be used to contradict clear text, but it often resolves ambiguity and shows why one reading fits the legislation better.

---

## Module 5: Harmonious Reading
A strong interpretation makes the statute work as a whole.

### Practical tests
- Does this reading make another provision pointless?
- Does it create internal contradiction?
- Does it nullify an express protection elsewhere?
- Does it reward an end-run the Act appears designed to prevent?

### Example use
If one reading of the RTA permits a landlord to avoid a tenant protection by relabelling a fee or occupancy arrangement, that reading may be inconsistent with anti-avoidance features of the scheme.

---

## Module 6: Consequences and Absurdity
Courts are cautious about interpretations that produce outcomes that are irrational, incoherent, or plainly contrary to the statute’s object.

### Useful consequence arguments
- the other reading defeats the protective function of the Act
- the other reading creates arbitrary distinctions
- the other reading makes compliance impossible or meaningless
- the other reading turns an exception into the rule

### Warning
Do not call every harsh result “absurd.” Reserve that label for genuinely incoherent outcomes.

---

## Module 7: Common Presumptions and Canons
Use these as tools, not magic spells.

### Ejusdem generis
Where general words follow specific ones, the general words may be limited by the nature of the specific class.

### Expressio unius arguments
Where the legislature expressly included one thing, omission of another may sometimes matter. Use carefully.

### Presumption against tautology
Assume the legislature did not add words for no reason.

### Presumption of consistent expression
The same term used across the Act may carry a consistent meaning unless context says otherwise.

### Presumption against absurdity
Interpretation should avoid ridiculous or self-defeating results where the text allows a coherent alternative.

### Remedial interpretation
Protective or remedial legislation may be read generously in a manner consistent with its purpose, but still within the statutory language.

---

## Module 8: Administrative-Law Overlay
When a tribunal interpreted the statute, the question may not be “what is the best interpretation in the abstract?” but rather:
- what are the plausible interpretations?
- did the decision-maker engage the text, context, and purpose?
- is the interpretation justified and coherent?

### Vavilov discipline
A reviewing court will examine whether the reasons demonstrate a rational chain from statutory language and context to result.

### Key question
Even if another interpretation is stronger, is the tribunal’s interpretation outside the range of reasonable outcomes?

---

## Module 9: Transitional and Temporal Issues
Always test whether the provision:
- changed over time,
- was amended after the events,
- has transitional rules,
- or uses timing language that affects retroactivity.

This is especially important in tenancy, procedural, and administrative regimes that change frequently.

---

## Module 10: Anti-Avoidance and End-Run Detection
This skill should be aggressive at spotting form-over-substance arguments.

### Watch for
- labels used to defeat statutory protection
- "fees" that function as prohibited charges
- occupancy arrangements dressed up to avoid tenant status
- procedural shortcuts that erase mandatory notice rights
- broad discretion arguments that hollow out explicit safeguards

### Working rule
If a proposed interpretation makes statutory rights vanish through relabelling, test whether it is inconsistent with the Act’s structure and protective purpose.

## Output Formats
- provision analysis memo
- competing-interpretations table
- statute-to-facts matrix
- purpose/scheme note
- anti-avoidance argument section
- judicial review statutory-reasonableness critique

---

## Model Argument Template
### ISSUE
What does section X permit, prohibit, or require?

### TEXT
Set out the words actually used.

### CONTEXT / SCHEME
Explain where the section sits and how neighbouring provisions operate.

### PURPOSE
Explain the statutory object engaged.

### APPLICATION
Apply the preferred reading to the actual facts and procedural setting.

### RESPONSE TO OTHER SIDE
Identify why the competing reading is too narrow, too broad, internally inconsistent, or contrary to scheme/purpose.

### CONCLUSION
State the interpretation advanced and the resulting order or consequence.

---

## Quality Review
Before finalizing:
- Have you read the whole relevant part, not just one section?
- Did you check definitions, regulations, and transitional provisions?
- Did you separate best interpretation from reasonable interpretation?
- Are you relying on consequence rhetoric without textual anchor?
- Did you test whether the other side’s reading creates an end-run around the statute?
- If this is JR, did you analyze the tribunal’s reasons and not just the provision in the abstract?
