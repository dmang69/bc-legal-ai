Locked design guardrails

These six corrections are locked into the product design and enforced across the private backend. They are displayed here so the design posture is auditable.

1 · Consent is not privilege

Client consent authorizes specified data processing. It never automatically creates, waives, or determines solicitor–client or litigation privilege. Privilege analysis is a separate, human-supervised determination.

2 · Consent withdrawal is not unconditional deletion

Under BC's PIPA, withdrawal of consent generally operates on reasonable notice, and processing may continue where authorized without consent or required by legal obligations. The engine distinguishes:
  • Immediate: revocation of future optional AI access — takes effect right away.
  • Separately assessed: retention schedules, legal holds, and evidentiary obligations — never auto-deleted by a consent toggle.

4 · The JR clock is 60 days from issuance — with alternatives

The ordinary RTB judicial-review limitation is 60 days from issuance of the final decision (ATA s.57(1)), subject to the extension power and statutory criteria in ATA s.57(2). When finality, issuance date, or enabling legislation is uncertain, the engine must calculate and display alternative deadlines, never a single confident date. Try the JR clock tab.

5 · Honest encryption claims

True end-to-end encryption is incompatible with server-side AI analysis of the same content. The architecture commits to one of two honest postures: on-device classification (content never leaves the device unencrypted), or controlled server-side decryption with explicit consent, disclosure, audit logging, and scoped access. It will never claim "inaccessible-to-server E2EE" while performing unrestricted server-side AI processing. (This demo page itself is the first posture: everything runs in your browser.)

6 · The RTB archive is not a complete corpus

The official RTB decision archive covers specific historical publication ranges and categories. Absence of a decision from the archive is never treated as proof that no decision exists. Search results are labelled as drawn from the published subset, and negative results are reported as "not found in the published subset" — not "does not exist."

    uvicorn backend.api.main:app --port 8000

    • POST /v1/matters/{id}/consents — purpose-specific grant
    • POST /v1/consents/evaluate-operation — processing-basis evaluation
